      ones (maybe that’s just my inherent mistrust of authority). Centralized systems are easier to control,
      there’s faster decision making and rapid response, and they can eliminate duplicated efforts. But
      the risk of group think, rigidity and blind spots is really high. Decentralization is messier. There
      are fits and starts. Abandoned ideas. Arguments. But this is where innovation comes from. When there
      are a lot of competing ideas, new thinking emerges and the winning ideas can rise to the top. Of
      course, better outcomes aren’t guaranteed but the potential for solving problems in novel ways is
      really high. I think one perception about collectivism is that it waters down the brilliance of
      an “exceptional individual”. There’s a fear that if a genius’s ideas are forced to go through the
      de-flavorizing machine of a million opinions, nothing truly special or interesting ever gets produced.

      of open source principles: collaboration, transparency, inclusive meritocracy. I’ve seen over and

      brilliant idea and someone else can look at it from their unique perspective and to add to it. Or
      challenge it. Brilliant people pushing other brilliant people, not diluting their ideas. Now, it’s
